# Realism Standard

## Visual target
Photorealistic live-action realism with believable Brazilian human characteristics and individual identity.

## Human realism
Use natural variation rather than synthetic perfection:
- facial asymmetry
- realistic skin texture and pores when appropriate
- subtle freckles, marks and pigmentation
- natural under-eye variation
- realistic lips and teeth
- believable hair density and individual strands
- natural baby hairs and flyaways
- realistic eyebrows
- plausible hands and anatomy
- natural body proportions
- subtle expression asymmetry
- realistic posture and micro-movements

Avoid porcelain/plastic skin, excessive symmetry, beauty-filter appearance, generic influencer faces, exaggerated eyes, impossible jawlines, over-smoothed skin, CGI appearance, artificial HDR and generic AI beauty aesthetics.

## Brazilian character design
Brazilian identity is culturally and phenotypically diverse. Do not reduce it to one fixed appearance. Each character receives a specific visual identity based on biography, narrative context and, when relevant, regional/family background.

## Beauty is optional
Not every character should be conventionally attractive. Ordinary, distinctive and visibly imperfect faces are valid and often desirable for realism.

## Photography target
Natural daylight, practical lighting, believable exposure, realistic shadows, natural lens rendering, subtle grain, realistic skin response, environmental reflections and authentic backgrounds.

## Identity
Realism never overrides the Identity Lock.

## Approval benchmark
Reject anything that reads as plastic, CGI, overprocessed, generic AI portrait, anatomically implausible, culturally stereotyped or inconsistent with canon.

Target: a frame plausibly photographed in a professional live-action production.
