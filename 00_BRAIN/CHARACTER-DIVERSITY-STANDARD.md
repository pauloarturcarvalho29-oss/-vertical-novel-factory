# Character Diversity Standard

## Principle

The cast must represent believable human diversity.

Characters are not required to be conventionally attractive, symmetrical, young, thin or visually idealized.

Every character must have an individual identity.

## Brazilian realism

"Brazilian" is not a single phenotype.

Character design may reflect the country's broad diversity of:

- skin tones
- facial structures
- hair textures and patterns
- eye shapes and colors
- body types
- heights
- ages
- regional backgrounds
- family histories
- socioeconomic contexts
- cultural environments

The system must not use stereotypes as shortcuts for Brazilian identity.

## Beauty is not a production requirement

Characters may be:

- conventionally attractive
- ordinary-looking
- distinctive-looking
- asymmetrical
- older
- younger
- thin
- muscular
- heavy
- short
- tall
- scarred
- freckled
- visibly aged
- physically imperfect

The story determines how appearance matters.

## Individuality

Each recurring character should have a recognizable combination of:

- face geometry
- skin characteristics
- hair
- body proportions
- age
- posture
- facial expressions
- voice
- mannerisms
- wardrobe
- personal objects
- cultural/social context

Two characters should not accidentally share the same generic AI face.

## Natural imperfections

Appropriate imperfections are encouraged when consistent with the character:

- facial asymmetry
- acne or acne scars
- freckles
- moles
- wrinkles
- expression lines
- uneven teeth
- distinctive nose shape
- distinctive ears
- hair irregularities
- scars
- stretch marks
- natural body variation

These are identity features, not defects to automatically remove.

## Anti-template rule

Never construct the cast by repeatedly applying the same "beautiful Brazilian" prompt.

The Character Director must generate each identity independently from the narrative requirements.

## Continuity

Once a character is approved, their individual traits become canonical.

Natural realism cannot be used as an excuse for identity drift.

## QA

Reject a character if:

- it is a generic copy of another character
- its appearance contradicts its canonical profile
- its features are excessively idealized without narrative reason
- it relies on ethnic stereotypes
- it lacks enough distinctive traits to remain identifiable across shots
