# Character Diversity Standard

## Principle
The cast must represent believable human diversity. Characters are not required to be conventionally attractive, symmetrical, young, thin or visually idealized.

## Brazilian realism
"Brazilian" is not a single phenotype. Character design may reflect the country's broad diversity of skin tones, facial structures, hair textures, eye characteristics, body types, ages, regional backgrounds, family histories and cultural environments.

Never use stereotypes as shortcuts for Brazilian identity.

## Beauty is not a production requirement
Characters may be conventionally attractive, ordinary-looking, distinctive-looking, asymmetrical, older, younger, thin, muscular, heavy, short, tall, scarred, freckled or visibly aged.

## Individuality
Each recurring character should have a recognizable combination of face geometry, skin, hair, body proportions, age, posture, expressions, voice, mannerisms, wardrobe and personal context.

Two characters must not accidentally share the same generic AI face.

## Natural imperfections
Appropriate imperfections are encouraged when canonical: facial asymmetry, acne/scars, freckles, moles, wrinkles, expression lines, uneven teeth, distinctive ears/nose, hair irregularities, scars, stretch marks and natural body variation.

## Anti-template rule
Never construct the cast by repeatedly applying the same "beautiful Brazilian" prompt.

## Continuity
Once approved, individual traits become canonical. Realism never excuses identity drift.

## QA
Reject a character if it is a generic copy, contradicts canon, is excessively idealized without narrative reason, relies on stereotypes, or lacks enough distinctive traits to remain identifiable.
